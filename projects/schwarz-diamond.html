01 · Concept

Minimal Surface &
Structural Logic

The Schwarz Diamond belongs to the family of triply periodic minimal surfaces — geometries that divide space into two congruent, intertwined labyrinths, each mirroring the diamond bond structure. First described by mathematician H. A. Schwarz in the 1880s and later named by Alan Schoen, the surface is defined by a precise implicit equation and possesses the rare property of being able to periodically repeat itself in space through its four bases.

In this workshop, the Schwarz Diamond surface served as both the conceptual and structural point of departure. The goal was to translate this mathematical geometry into a physically buildable bending-active structure — a form that holds itself through material tension rather than rigid connections.

Step 01

Create a regular triangle pyramid as the base geometry

Step 02

Scale the second pyramid relative to the gravity point of the first

Step 03

Form 12 triangular meshes between the corresponding edges of both pyramids

Step 04

Apply force to the mesh until the structure relaxes into its minimal form

Step 05

Convert each mesh face into a buildable, numbered flat part for fabrication

02 · Fabrication

From Computation
to Physical Form

Each mesh face was individually numbered, optimally packed using OpenNest in Grasshopper, and laser-cut from flat sheet material. The pieces were then assembled by hand, with the structure's integrity maintained entirely through bending tension — no glue, no rigid fasteners. The result is a lightweight, intricate form that echoes the mathematical elegance of its source geometry.

The project highlights the full pipeline from abstract parametric logic to physical artifact: surface definition, mesh relaxation, part numbering, nesting optimization, laser cutting, and manual assembly. Each step demanded precision, and the final structure carries the traces of every decision made along the way.
